## What are Workspaces?

Workspaces are collaborative spaces that group applications and databases and share them with a
team. Creating one requires the **Enterprise** plan; the maximum number of members and custom
roles both scale with plan tier.

## Permissions, not fixed roles

A member doesn't hold a fixed role like "Admin" or "Viewer" — they hold a **role you define**,
and a role is just a name plus a chosen subset of **19 permissions**. Four roles are seeded when
the workspace is created (Admin, Developer, Operator, Viewer), but they're ordinary roles from
that point on: rename them, edit their permissions, or delete them like any other.

The **owner** isn't a role either. Ownership is possession of the workspace itself: the owner
always has every permission, without needing a role assignment, and only the owner can rename or
delete the workspace, transfer ownership, or link/unlink an application or database.

### Permission catalog

| Group | Permissions |
|---|---|
| Apps | `apps:read`, `apps:manage`, `apps:lifecycle`, `apps:envs`, `apps:files`, `apps:delete` |
| Databases | `databases:read`, `databases:manage`, `databases:lifecycle`, `databases:credentials`, `databases:data`, `databases:delete` |
| Snapshots | `snapshots:read`, `snapshots:manage` |
| Members | `members:read`, `members:manage` |
| Roles | `roles:manage` |
| Activities | `activities:read` |

`apps:delete` and `databases:delete` are split out from `apps:manage`/`databases:manage` on
purpose — deleting is the one action worth gating separately from day-to-day management, which
is also why the seeded **Admin** role doesn't include `snapshots:manage` by default (restoring
or deleting a snapshot is destructive enough that the owner opts in explicitly).

<Note>
  A member can't create or edit a role with permissions they don't themselves hold — including
  the owner's exclusive actions, which no role can ever grant. This closes the obvious escalation
  path: `roles:manage` lets you shape roles, not hand out access above your own.
</Note>

## Custom roles

Create as many roles as your plan's tier allows (the four seed roles count toward that cap), pick
any subset of the 19 permissions.

A role that's assigned to a member or has a pending invite can't be deleted
(`WORKSPACE_ROLE_IN_USE`) — reassign or revoke first.

## Inviting members

Invites replace the old single shared code entirely:

- **By e-mail** — sent to a specific address, expires in 7 days, one pending invite per address.
  Re-inviting the same address refreshes and resends it.
- **By link** — a shareable URL, valid 24 hours, optionally capped at a number of uses.

Both kinds assign a role up front and can carry an **access expiry**: the member who joins
through that invite automatically loses access on that date, unless you set one later.
Everything here — sending, listing, revoking — is a dashboard-only action; there's no API-key
equivalent, so a leaked key can't add or remove people.

See the full route reference in [API Reference → Workspaces → Invites](/api-reference/endpoint/workspaces/invites/create).

## Requesting sensitive actions

A member who wants to do something they don't have the permission for — delete an application,
delete a database, create or restore a snapshot — can **request** it instead. Anyone holding the
matching permission can approve or reject the request from the workspace's **Requests** tab.
Approving re-runs the action for real, as the approver, using exactly the parameters that were
submitted with the request — never anything resent at approval time. A request expires after 24
hours unresolved.

## Activity

The **Activities** tab is scoped to the whole workspace instead of just your own actions, with a
CSV export capped at a 90-day window per download.

## Folders and favorites

A workspace's folders and favorites are **individual** preferences. Each member organizes the
applications and databases they can access in their own view; creating a folder, changing its
name or color, moving a resource, and favoriting don't change other members' organization.

Folders can contain applications and databases and use the colors `neutral`, `red`, `orange`,
`yellow`, `green`, `blue` and `purple`. Favorites are independent from folders. These preferences
are saved on the account, either personal-scoped or workspace-scoped, not in the browser's local
storage.

The same actions can be done through the dashboard, through workflows, or through clients that use
an API key, such as the CLI, MCP, and the VS Code extension. Each action generates an activity in
the history; the client doesn't need to send the origin or the author.

<Note>
  A folder and a Flow group are different concepts. The folder organizes applications and
  databases on the account; the group organizes elements on Flow's local canvas. There's no sync
  or migration between them.
</Note>

## Frozen workspaces

If the owner's plan expires or drops below Enterprise, the workspace is **frozen, not deleted**:
members, projects and roles all stay, but writes are refused — renaming, inviting,
changing a role, linking a project. Reading, leaving, removing a member or a project, and
deleting the workspace itself keep working. Everything unfreezes the moment the plan is
current again; what's checked is the **owner's** plan, so a Free-plan member of someone else's
paid workspace is unaffected.
