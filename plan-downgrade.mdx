## How Downgrades Work

A downgrade on Vertra Cloud takes effect **immediately**: your account moves to the **plan right below** the current one and **keeps its expiration date**. The difference for the remaining days does not become credit, additional time or a refund, and your next renewal is charged at the price of the smaller plan.

<Tip>
  The less time is left until your expiration date, the less of the bigger plan you leave unused. To make the most of what you already paid, downgrade close to your expiration date.
</Tip>

<Info>
  Downgrades always move to the **immediately previous plan** in the hierarchy. You cannot skip plans.
</Info>

Before you confirm, the dashboard shows the new plan and your expiration date.

## Expiration Date and Renewal

- **The expiration date does not change.** You keep the days you already paid for, now on the smaller plan.
- **The next renewal is on the smaller plan,** at its price and in the same billing cycle.
- **An automatic renewal already created for the old plan is cancelled.** The next one is created on the smaller plan. If that renewal is being charged at that very moment, the downgrade asks you to wait a few seconds and try again.
- **No credit, no extra days, no refund** for the difference between the two plans.

## Plan Hierarchy

```
STARTER 1 → STARTER 2 → PRO 4 → PRO 6 → PRO 8 → BUSINESS 12 → BUSINESS 16 → BUSINESS 24 → ENTERPRISE 32 → ... → ENTERPRISE 512
```

<Warning>
  The **STARTER 1** plan cannot be downgraded further.
</Warning>

<Note>
  Downgrades only happen from **Account Settings**. The checkout never accepts a plan smaller
  than your current one — the plan picker hides them, and `POST /v1/orders` answers
  `400 NOT_AN_UPGRADE` for any order type that would shrink the plan.
</Note>

## Before You Downgrade

The system will check the following before processing:

<Steps>
  <Step title="Memory usage">
    Your total memory usage across all apps and databases must fit within the target plan's memory limit. If it exceeds, you'll need to reduce resource allocation first.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Docker images">
    The Docker images you have (your own and the ones image templates take, one per distinct image) must fit within the image slots of the target plan. If they exceed, delete images or image template apps first.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Rate limit">
    You can only downgrade once every **3 hours** to prevent abuse.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Email confirmation">
    The dashboard asks for a code sent to your email before it downgrades the plan.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## What Changes on Downgrade

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Resources & Limits">
    - Memory limit is reduced to the target plan's allocation
    - Apps and databases that exceed the new limit must be resized beforehand
    - CPU allocation is adjusted proportionally
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Features">
    - Features exclusive to higher plans are immediately disabled
    - For example: zero-downtime deploys, the database data manager, etc.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Domains, members and roles">
    If you have more custom domains, workspace members or roles than the smaller plan allows, the ones you already have stay, but you cannot add new ones until you are back within the limit.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Workspaces">
    If you go from **Starter 2** to **Starter 1**, which has no workspaces, your workspaces are **frozen, not deleted**: members, projects and roles stay, and you can still read, leave, remove members or projects and delete the workspace. Changes such as renaming, inviting, changing a role or linking a project are refused until your plan is Starter 2 or above again. See [Frozen workspaces](/workspaces#frozen-workspaces).
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## Deploy History on Downgrade

When you downgrade, the earlier versions of your applications beyond the new plan's limit stop being available for rollback right away. Their code is deleted shortly after. Upgrading again does not bring back code that was already deleted. The version that is running is never affected.

See [Deploy history and rollback limits](/plans-and-limits#deploy-history-and-rollback-limits) for how many versions each plan keeps.

## Deploy Settings on Downgrade

Your application's automatic rollback and zero-downtime settings are not erased when you downgrade. The saved value stays on the application, but it only takes effect on a plan that includes the feature: automatic rollback from Pro, zero-downtime deploys from Starter 2. If you upgrade again, the saved values act again. While you are below the required plan, you can still turn either setting off, but not on.

## How to Downgrade

1. Go to **Dashboard → Settings → Account**
2. Scroll to the **Downgrade Plan** section
3. Review the new plan and your expiration date, then confirm with the code sent to your email

<Note>
  Downgrading is a dashboard-only action: a request made with an API key is refused with `403 API_KEY_SCOPE_DENIED`, whatever its scopes.
</Note>

## If the Downgrade Is Refused

- `DOWNGRADE_NOT_AVAILABLE` — you are already on STARTER 1
- `INSUFFICIENT_MEMORY_FOR_DOWNGRADE` — reduce resource usage first
- `INSUFFICIENT_IMAGE_SLOTS_FOR_DOWNGRADE` — the lower plan holds fewer Docker images than you use (image templates count): delete images or image template apps first
- `429` — rate limited, wait 3 hours

Moving to a bigger plan works differently: see [How do I change my plan?](/plans-and-limits#how-do-i-change-my-plan).
