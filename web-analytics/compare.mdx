Compare puts the period you are looking at next to the one right before it, so you can tell whether a number is good or bad for your site. It is available on every plan.

Same data through the API: [Application traffic](/api-reference/endpoint/apps/analytics/traffic). Send `compare=true`, and `compare_start` to choose where the compared period starts.

## How it works

Turn on **Compare** in the period bar. The previous period is the same length as the selected one, immediately before it:

| Selected | Compared with |
|---|---|
| Last 24 hours | The 24 hours before that |
| Last 7 days | The 7 days before that |
| A custom range | A range of the same length right before it |

You can also choose where the compared period starts. It always has the same length as the selected one:

| Compare with | Starts at |
|---|---|
| Previous period | Right before the selected one (the default) |
| Previous week | 7 days before the selected one starts |
| Custom | The date and time you pick |

A compared period that ends after the selected one starts is not accepted, because the two would overlap.

Two things change on screen:

- **Charts** draw the previous period as a dashed line over the current one, aligned by position in time.
- **Numbers** show the change, such as "+12%" for requests or a lower response time, next to the value.

## Reading the change

- More requests is not automatically good or bad: it depends on whether you launched something or a bot arrived.
- **Response time and error rate going up** is the part to act on. Check [Errors](/web-analytics/errors) and [Performance](/web-analytics/performance).
- A very small base exaggerates percentages: from 2 errors to 4 is "+100%". Look at the counts too.

## Limits

The compared period must also fit in the history of your plan; see [History and plans](/web-analytics/history-and-plans). Compare keeps working with filters: the same filters apply to both periods.
